## Evidence-backed corrections

### HLG-CONF-015 — Infant-baptism families incorrectly categorized as separate from sacramental efficacy

- **Severity:** Minor / theological-confessional precision.
- **Location at parent commit:** `src/content/works/how-to-love-god/chapters/15-gathered-worship-baptism-and-the-lord-s-supper.md:30`, “Baptism”.
- **Original:** Protestants disagree about the proper subjects of baptism, its mode, and how to describe its efficacy. Baptists emphasize believer's baptism and often speak of ordinance and testimony; paedobaptist traditions baptize believers and their children within covenantal frameworks; sacramental traditions use stronger language of means and divine action. Those differences are substantial and should not be hidden.
- **Why:** The taxonomy implies that infant-baptizing traditions are uniformly framed by covenant theology and contrasts them with 'sacramental traditions' that emphasize means and divine action. Lutherans expressly teach infant baptism as a means through which grace is offered, while the Reformed confession expressly treats baptism as a covenant sign and seal, including infants. These are intersecting categories, not mutually exclusive denominations.
- **Primary evidence:** [SBC Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/); [Westminster Confession 27.1–3 and 28.1–4](https://wts.edu/wcf); [Augsburg Confession IX](https://bookofconcord.cph.org/en/augsburg-confession/chief_articles/article_ix/); [Augsburg Confession X](https://sfg.ekd.de/english/augsburg_confession.html).
- **Correction:** Protestants disagree about the subjects and mode of baptism and how to describe its efficacy. Many Baptists baptize professing believers and describe baptism as an ordinance that publicly testifies to faith in Christ. Reformed paedobaptists also baptize believers' children and describe baptism as a covenant sign and seal. Lutherans likewise baptize infants but explicitly teach that God offers grace through baptism. These are distinct confessional accounts, not mutually exclusive categories of infant baptism and sacramental efficacy. The differences are substantial and should not be hidden.
- **Scope:** No change to a baptismal conclusion, reader injunction, release metadata, or church-admission policies.

### HLG-SAFE-017 — Acute self-harm danger lacks a distinct urgent pathway

- **Severity:** Major / pastoral safety omission (original existing clinical referral was appropriate for non-imminent presentations).
- **Location at parent commit:** `src/content/works/how-to-love-god/chapters/17-when-god-feels-distant.md:44`, “Depression, anhedonia, and other clinical factors”.
- **Original:** Persistent low mood, significant loss of interest, major changes in functioning, severe anxiety, self-harm thoughts, or other serious symptoms warrant appropriate professional assessment. Seeking such help is not an admission that spiritual realities do not matter. It recognizes that human beings are embodied creatures and that competent care can address dimensions pastors are not trained to diagnose.
- **Why:** A general recommendation for 'professional assessment' does not clearly differentiate routine clinical help from danger of acting imminently on suicidal/self-harm thoughts. Clinical and public-health guidance calls for immediate crisis/emergency services in imminent danger. Readers must not infer that waiting for a normal appointment suffices.
- **Primary evidence:** [WHO, Suicide: what to do in immediate danger](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/suicide); [NIMH, Frequently Asked Questions About Suicide](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/suicide-faq); [NHS, Help for suicidal thoughts](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/behaviours/help-for-suicidal-thoughts/). WHO/NIMH/NHS national phone numbers differ; the manuscript uses location-independent 'local emergency or crisis assistance'.
- **Correction:** Persistent low mood, significant loss of interest, major changes in functioning, severe anxiety, self-harm thoughts, or other serious symptoms warrant appropriate professional assessment. **If someone is at immediate risk of acting on suicidal or self-harm thoughts, seek local emergency or crisis assistance immediately rather than waiting for a routine appointment.** Seeking professional help is not an admission that spiritual realities do not matter. It recognizes that human beings are embodied creatures and that competent care can address dimensions pastors are not trained to diagnose.
- **Source apparatus:** Expand existing `Appendix L:80` endnote 17.3 rather than create a duplicate out-of-order note; add WHO guidance to `Appendix L` evidence bibliography.
- **Scope:** No individual risk diagnosis, medication/treatment instructions, or claim that all intrusive thoughts imply suicidal intent.
