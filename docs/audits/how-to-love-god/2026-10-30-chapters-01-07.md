## Supported corrections implemented in this PR

### HLG-EXE-006 — Chapter 6, insufficient qualification of John 3:5

- **Severity:** Minor (substantive exegetical omission; no original contradiction established).
- **Location at source commit:** `src/content/works/how-to-love-god/chapters/06-new-life-in-christ.md:24`, section `New birth and divine initiative`.
- **Original:** `Jesus tells Nicodemus that entrance into God's kingdom requires birth from above or new birth and connects this work with the Spirit (John 3:1–8). The image emphasizes dependence. A person does not cause his own birth.`
- **Why:** The cited passage includes Jesus' explicit wording `water and the Spirit` in John 3:5. Omitting it from an otherwise detailed regeneration discussion obscures a material interpretive question, particularly for a book addressing readers across Protestant traditions.
- **Evidence:** [John 3:3–8 NET](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A3-8&version=NET), [Ezekiel 36:25–27 NET](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezekiel+36%3A25-27&version=NET), [NET Bible John 3:5 explanatory note](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php/verse.php?book=Joh&chapter=3&tab=commentaries&verse=5), and [exegetical literature discussing baptismal and cleansing interpretations](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S2074-77052022000100064&script=sci_arttext).
- **Minimal change:** `Jesus tells Nicodemus that entrance into God's kingdom requires birth from above or new birth and connects this work with the Spirit (John 3:1–8). Verse 5 also speaks of being born “of water and the Spirit.” Interpreters connect that wording with Old Testament cleansing and renewal imagery (compare Ezekiel 36:25–27), with baptism, or with both in different ways. The central point here—that entry into God's kingdom depends on divine renewal—does not require settling those differences. The birth image emphasizes dependence: a person does not cause his own birth.`
- **Scope:** Explicitly states the verse's wording and a real interpretive range; does not assert one baptismal theology as universally binding.

### HLG-CONF-007 — Chapter 7, overbroad four-lens model attribution

- **Severity:** Minor (confessional attribution/precision; framework itself is not identified as doctrinally false).
- **Location at source commit:** `src/content/works/how-to-love-god/chapters/07-accepted-in-christ-justification-adoption-and-assurance.md:32`, section `Four lenses for assurance`.
- **Original:** `Assurance is often damaged by forcing every question through a single lens. A more balanced Protestant model uses four related lenses while keeping them in the right order.`
- **Why:** The four-lens taxonomy is editorial. It should not be presented as a recognizable standard shared by Protestant confessions. Westminster Confession XVIII.2 groups promise, inward graces and Spirit's testimony; Heidelberg Catechism Q21 emphasizes faith, Christ's merits and Spirit's work through the gospel; Augsburg Confession IV–VI emphasizes Christ, the Word/sacraments and good fruits, with distinct Lutheran terminology. Wesleyan statements also have their own emphases.
- **Evidence:** [Westminster Confession XVIII](https://www.wts.edu/wcf), [Heidelberg Catechism Q21 at EKD](https://www.ekd.de/en/Heidelberg-Catechism-302.htm), [Augsburg Confession IV–VI](https://els.org/beliefs/augsburgconfession/), [Wesleyan Discipline doctrine of assurance](https://discipline.wesleyan.org/wiki/Assurance%3A_Doctrine_of).
- **Minimal change:** `Assurance is often damaged by forcing every question through a single lens. For practical clarity, this chapter groups several biblical and Protestant confessional emphases into four related lenses. This is an editorial framework, not a formula shared identically by all Protestant traditions.`
- **Scope:** Preserve all four lenses, later application, and pastoral safeguards; clarify that they organize the book rather than constitute a uniform historical confession.
