# Glossary

**Agent** — An AI-enabled system that can use tools, maintain state, and perform sequences of actions rather than only generate a single response.

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** — A broad practical term for computational systems that perform tasks associated with prediction, classification, generation, recognition, or decision support.

**Back-translation** — Translation of a target-language draft back into a source or reference language to help evaluate meaning.

**Digital colonialism** — A pattern in which technological infrastructure, data, standards, or cultural assumptions are controlled externally in ways that reduce local agency.

**Generative AI** — AI systems designed to produce new content such as text, images, audio, video, or code.

**Hallucination** — A fluent but false or unsupported AI-generated output.

**Large language model (LLM)** — A machine-learning model trained on large text corpora to predict and generate language.

**Localization** — Adaptation of content or software for a particular linguistic and cultural context beyond literal translation.

**Low-resource language** — A language with relatively limited digitized text, speech data, tools, models, or computational resources.

**Multimodal AI** — Systems that can process or generate more than one data type, such as text, images, audio, or video.

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** — A system design in which an AI model receives retrieved documents or data as context before generating a response.

**Synthetic media** — Machine-generated or machine-modified text, images, audio, or video that may imitate human-created media.
