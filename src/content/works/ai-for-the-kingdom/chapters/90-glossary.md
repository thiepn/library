**Agent.** An AI system capable of pursuing a goal through multiple steps, often using external tools or taking actions rather than merely generating a response.

**Artificial intelligence (AI).** A broad category of computational systems that perform tasks associated with capabilities such as language generation, prediction, classification, perception, or decision support.

**Confabulation.** A confidently presented but erroneous or fabricated generative-AI output; used in this book particularly in connection with NIST terminology.

**Data minimization.** Reducing the amount and sensitivity of information collected, transmitted, or stored to what is actually necessary.

**Digital colonialism.** A diagnostic term for technological relationships involving patterns such as extraction, durable dependency, weak local control, externally imposed norms, or poor exit capacity.

**Generative AI.** AI systems designed to produce outputs such as text, images, audio, video, or code.

**Great Commission.** The Christian commission to make disciples, associated especially with Matthew 28:18–20 and interpreted within the broader New Testament missionary witness.

**Human handoff.** A designed point where an automated system transfers responsibility or interaction to an accountable person.

**Imago Dei.** The biblical and theological doctrine that human beings are created in the image of God.

**Large language model (LLM).** A computational model trained on large bodies of language data to predict and generate sequences of language.

**Least privilege.** The security principle that a system should receive only the permissions necessary to perform its intended task.

**Local AI.** AI processing performed primarily on a local device or locally controlled infrastructure rather than through an external cloud service.

**Low-resource language.** A language with limited digital or computational resources for a particular technological task; the term describes resource availability, not linguistic worth.

**Missio Dei.** A theological term referring to mission as originating in God's own action rather than being invented or possessed by the Church.

**Multimodal AI.** AI capable of processing or generating more than one type of information, such as text, speech, images, or video.

**People group.** A contemporary missiological category used to describe a socially meaningful population for strategic analysis.

**Prompt injection.** An attack or failure mode in which instructions embedded in untrusted material manipulate an AI system's behavior.

**Provenance.** Information about the origin, history, creation, or modification of content.

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** A system design in which retrieved external information is supplied to a generative model as context for its answer.

**Synthetic authority.** Authority attributed to an AI system because of its apparent knowledge, responsiveness, or relational competence rather than through legitimate human or ecclesial accountability.

**Synthetic media.** Images, audio, video, or other media substantially created by generative systems.

**Unreached people group.** A strategic mission category used by mission-research organizations according to defined demographic and Christian-presence thresholds; definitions differ among organizations.

**Verification.** The process of checking an AI output or other claim against appropriate evidence or qualified judgment.
