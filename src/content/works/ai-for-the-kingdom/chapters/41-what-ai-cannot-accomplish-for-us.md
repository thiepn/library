# What AI Cannot Accomplish for Us

Artificial intelligence may become more capable than we currently imagine.

It may translate better.

Reason better.

Write better.

Program better.

Teach more effectively.

Coordinate increasingly complex work.

Christians should still know what capability cannot produce.

AI cannot make the Church faithful.

It cannot make missionaries willing to go.

It cannot create love for enemies.

It cannot produce holiness through computation.

It cannot make believers courageous under persecution.

It cannot replace prayer.

It cannot create repentance.

It cannot reconcile sinners to God.

It cannot become the body of Christ.

It cannot pour out the Holy Spirit.

The missionary task has always depended on divine power working through human weakness.

This truth protects Christians from technological anxiety.

If AI becomes extraordinarily capable, the Church's identity remains.

If AI progress slows, the Church's mission remains.

If every digital system disappeared tomorrow, Christians would still possess the gospel, the Scriptures, prayer, fellowship, baptism, the Lord's Supper, and the command to make disciples.

Technology is useful because it serves these realities.

It is never their foundation.

The Church should therefore become technologically competent without becoming technologically dependent in spirit.

We can use excellent tools with open hands.

We can build boldly.

We can experiment.

We can learn.

And we can remember that the kingdom of God does not arrive through compute.

[^1]: This chapter distinguishes instrumental capability from the divine and ecclesial realities that Christian theology does not treat as technological outputs.
