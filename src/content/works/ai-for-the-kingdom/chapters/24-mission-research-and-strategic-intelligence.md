# Mission Research and Strategic Intelligence

Mission strategy depends on understanding reality.

Where are churches growing?

Which languages lack Scripture?

Where are people migrating?

Which communities remain isolated from Christian witness?

What political changes affect access?

What ministries already exist?

The volume of relevant information is enormous.

AI can help researchers process it.

Large collections of reports, datasets, articles, maps, and field notes can be searched and summarized.

Models can classify recurring themes, compare regions, identify gaps, and generate questions for deeper investigation.

This does not replace mission researchers.

It changes their leverage.

A researcher who previously reviewed fifty documents may review five hundred with AI assistance.

A strategy team can query decades of internal reports conversationally.

A mission agency can connect structured data to natural-language interfaces so leaders without technical training can explore it.

But strategic intelligence carries two dangers.

The first is false precision.

Mission data are often incomplete.

People-group categories differ between organizations.

Religious affiliation can be difficult to measure.

Church presence may be hidden.

Migration changes quickly.

AI may turn uncertain data into polished conclusions.

Researchers must preserve uncertainty.

The second danger is surveillance.

Detailed mission data can reveal vulnerable people.

Maps of house churches, leaders, converts, or ministry activity could become dangerous if exposed.

Not every useful dataset should exist.

Mission research should apply data minimization.

Collect what is needed.

Protect what is sensitive.

Avoid centralizing information whose compromise would harm people.

AI can strengthen strategic understanding.

But the Church should never become so data-hungry that people turn into coordinates.

Mission research exists to serve human beings and the spread of the gospel.

Data are a means.

People are not.

[^1]: Strategic datasets should preserve uncertainty labels and threat-sensitive access controls rather than presenting incomplete information as precise fact.
