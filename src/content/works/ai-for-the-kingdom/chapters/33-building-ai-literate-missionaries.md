# Building AI-Literate Missionaries

Future missionary training should include AI literacy.

Not because every missionary needs to become a machine-learning engineer.

They need enough understanding to use tools wisely.

AI literacy includes several skills.

Knowing how to write clear instructions.

Knowing that confident answers can be wrong.

Knowing how to verify sources.

Knowing what data are sensitive.

Knowing when a task requires an expert.

Knowing how to evaluate translation quality.

Knowing how to recognize automation risk.

Knowing how to preserve human responsibility.

Training should also include practical exercises.

Missionaries can compare AI summaries with original sources.

They can identify hallucinated citations.

They can practice adapting material for another culture and then ask local reviewers to critique it.

They can build small tools.

They can examine privacy policies.

They can simulate high-risk decisions and decide where human approval is required.

The goal is confidence without naïveté.

Missionaries should neither avoid AI because they do not understand it nor trust it because it appears intelligent.

The Church needs people who can use powerful systems while remaining spiritually and intellectually independent from them.

AI literacy will increasingly become part of ordinary vocational competence.

Mission training should prepare for that reality.

[^1]: Literacy should be updated as systems evolve; fixed prompt recipes age quickly, while verification, privacy, and responsibility principles remain durable.
