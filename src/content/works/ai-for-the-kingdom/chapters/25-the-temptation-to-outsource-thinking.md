# The Temptation to Outsource Thinking

AI makes thinking assistance effortless.

That convenience can become dependence.

A student asks AI before wrestling with a problem.

A pastor asks for a sermon outline before studying the text.

A missionary asks for cultural interpretation before talking to local friends.

A leader asks for a decision recommendation before clarifying the problem.

Each action seems harmless.

Repeated over time, they can change habits.

Human abilities grow through use.

Memory develops through remembering.

Reasoning develops through solving problems.

Writing develops through writing.

Discernment develops through judgment.

If AI constantly performs the difficult first step, users may lose the productive struggle through which competence forms.

The solution is not avoidance.

It is intentional sequencing.

Think first.

Then consult the tool.

Draft first.

Then ask for critique.

Study the passage first.

Then compare interpretations.

Attempt the language exercise first.

Then request correction.

This preserves human formation while gaining AI assistance.

Missionaries should be especially careful because field work requires adaptability.

Internet access may fail.

Tools may disappear.

Political restrictions may block services.

A missionary who cannot function without AI has become less resilient.

Technology should increase capability, not replace it.

There is also a spiritual dimension.

Thinking slowly can create space for prayer, reflection, and conviction.

Immediate generated answers may encourage impatience with ambiguity.

Not every difficult question needs instant resolution.

Christians should retain the ability to sit with Scripture, uncertainty, and complexity.

AI can become a powerful tutor.

A tutor should make the student stronger.

If the student becomes unable to work without the tutor, something has gone wrong.

[^1]: Learning science consistently distinguishes assistance that scaffolds practice from assistance that removes the need to practice; AI should be used in ways that preserve active cognitive work.
