Part I established what missionary preparation is under.

You are a disciple before you are a missionary. Scripture governs preparation without turning every modern method into divine command. Missionary direction should be discerned without demanding one universal calling experience. Serious preparation should remain connected to Christians who actually know you.

That still leaves the practical question: **What should you build when you do not yet know exactly what the future assignment will be?**

Uncertainty does not require inactivity.

Some preparation becomes valuable only when role, language, place, team, organization, and household situation are clearer. Other preparation travels well across almost any plausible missionary future.

Part II concentrates on that second category: character that remains valuable wherever you serve, real ministry practice where ministry responsibility is plausible, a durable vocation instead of a missionary-looking resume, and the ability to turn training into usable competence.

These foundations will not tell you where to go. They make you better prepared to carry whatever responsibility eventually becomes clear.
