Every prospective missionary is incomplete.

That fact tells us almost nothing about readiness.

The purpose of this chapter is to make preparation smaller again.

The question is whether your incompleteness matters enough, for this assignment, that deployment should wait.

## Five Planning Categories

This book uses five categories. They are not biblical categories, a psychological assessment, or a validated scoring instrument. They are a planning aid.

### Genuine blocker

A problem serious enough that deployment should ordinarily not proceed until it is adequately resolved or the assignment changes.

Possible examples include an unresolved serious character concern, a required professional qualification that is missing, an unsupported critical health need, inadequate language for immediately high-stakes responsibility, an unsafe organizational condition, or no lawful pathway for the intended role.

### Development need

A real weakness requiring growth but not necessarily delay.

Limited experience, weaker communication, incomplete theological knowledge, or immature planning habits may belong here depending on the role.

### Team or system responsibility

An essential capability that properly belongs outside the individual.

Specialist counseling, financial audit, crisis infrastructure, legal expertise, or particular safeguarding functions may fit this category.

### Field-learning item

Something best developed substantially through actual life in the context: nuanced cultural interpretation, advanced everyday language, local relationship networks, or contextual ministry judgment.

### Optional enhancement

Useful but unnecessary preparation: another course, extra certificate, secondary language unrelated to the role, or additional reading that does not resolve an actual responsibility.

## A Blocker Must Connect to Responsibility

Do not call something a blocker because it feels incomplete.

Ask what harm or failure the unresolved issue makes reasonably likely in this assignment.

If the answer is vague, the concern may not be a blocker.

Conversely, do not dismiss a serious issue because the candidate has many strengths.

Readiness does not average.

Excellent theology does not compensate for unsafe clinical practice. Strong language does not cancel unresolved character concerns. A healthy organization does not make a worker competent for a job they cannot do.

## Assignment Judgment, Not Spiritual Verdict

A deployment blocker is an assignment judgment, not a judgment on Christian worth or even necessarily on Christian maturity.

A licensing gap, medical limitation, unsupported family need, or inaccessible treatment may block one role without involving sin at all.

Likewise, a language limitation may be a blocker for unsupervised counseling, a development need for team participation, and a field-learning item for advanced cultural nuance.

Classification is role-relative and revisable.

## Beware Perfectionism

Perfectionism can disguise itself as seriousness.

The prospective missionary keeps adding requirements because departure feels frightening: more Bible knowledge, another year of ministry, higher language, more savings, another course.

The standard quietly becomes, "I will go when I no longer feel unready."

That day may never come.

Readiness is not absence of weakness.

It is sufficient preparation for responsibility.

## Beware False Urgency

The opposite error is, "Nobody is ever fully ready, so just go."

True premise.

Bad conclusion.

Nobody is perfectly ready. Some people are still clearly unready for a particular responsibility.

A person with unresolved serious abuse concerns should not be deployed because everyone is imperfect. A person lacking required professional competence should not practice because all professionals keep learning.

Field learning is not permission to export avoidable harm.

## Make the Categories Finite

For every serious gap, ask what category it is currently in, why, who is competent to judge it, what evidence would move it, and whether the assignment itself could change instead.

Appendix E provides the fuller worksheet.

You are no longer trying to become complete.

You are resolving what the actual responsibility requires.
