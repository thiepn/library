A missionary team may share the gospel, doctrine, prayer, sacrifice, and commitment to mission.

It can still experience serious conflict.

Shared faith does not erase personality, culture, communication habits, power, competing priorities, workload, theological differences, or family expectations.

Preparation should assume disagreement will occur.

The aim is not to avoid conflict.

It is to learn how conflict can remain truthful, accountable, and repairable.

## Define Expectations Before Crisis

Many team conflicts begin long before anyone becomes angry.

One person expects collaborative decisions. Another expects the leader to decide. One assumes weekends are protected. Another assumes ministry availability is constant. One expects direct disagreement. Another reads directness as disrespect. One sees hospitality as essential. Another experiences constant guests as household intrusion.

These differences should be discussed before they become moral judgments.

Ask early who decides what, who supervises whom, how feedback is given, how work hours are understood, what belongs to ministry and what belongs to personal time, what theological differences exist, what happens when someone disagrees with leadership, and how conflicts are mediated.

Explicit expectations are not evidence of distrust.

They reduce hidden assumptions.

## Unity Does Not Mean Agreement

Ephesians 4 calls Christians to preserve unity with humility, patience, truthfulness, and love.

That does not mean Christians will agree about every strategy.

Acts records serious disagreements among faithful workers.

Mission teams should reject the idea that visible conflict automatically proves spiritual failure.

The more dangerous pattern may be a team where nobody can disagree honestly.

Peace maintained through fear is not unity.

## Conflict That Can Be Repaired

Conflict competence includes naming the issue, listening, distinguishing intent from impact, admitting wrongdoing, changing behavior, forgiving appropriately, clarifying boundaries, and involving mediation when direct repair fails.

Matthew 18 may inform Christian confrontation, but its church-discipline context should not be reduced to a universal corporate HR procedure.

Modern organizations also need systems beyond ordinary interpersonal repair.

Some disputes exceed the "two people need to reconcile" category.

Allegations involving abuse, harassment, safeguarding, serious financial misconduct, retaliation, or abuse of supervisory authority should not be forced into an ordinary reconciliation process.

Chapter 18 will address the organizational structures required for those cases.

## Talk Before You Join

A prospective team should be able to describe how ordinary disagreement works.

How does a worker challenge a leader? Who mediates? What happens if mediation fails? How do cultural communication differences affect feedback? Can someone transfer teams? What happens when strategy disagreements become persistent?

Do not look for a team without conflict.

Look for one capable of truth, correction, repair, and accountability.

But even a healthy team is not the only community affected by missionary decisions.

For many workers, the assignment enters the home.
