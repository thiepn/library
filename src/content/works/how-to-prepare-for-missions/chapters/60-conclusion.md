You began this book with too much to prepare for.

Language. Theology. Character. Career. Church. Ministry. Culture. Team. Family. Health. Money. Organization. Risk. Calling.

Every subject seemed capable of becoming urgent.

The solution was never to discover which of them were unimportant.

The solution was to put them in order.

You are a disciple before you are a missionary.

That means missionary preparation begins under grace, Scripture, ordinary Christian faithfulness, prayer, and the church.

While your future remains unclear, build foundations that travel.

Grow in character. Serve. Develop real competence. Build a vocation. Learn how to learn.

When a plausible assignment begins to emerge, stop preparing for an imaginary universal missionary.

Define the responsibility.

Ask what you will actually do. Ask what competence the work requires. Ask which capabilities belong to you and which belong elsewhere in the body.

Then let the context make preparation specific: language, culture, power, team, household, sustainability, organization, money, risk.

Finally, stop adding requirements indiscriminately.

Identify what genuinely prevents responsible deployment. Allow other weaknesses to remain development needs. Let teams and specialists carry responsibilities that appropriately belong to them. Leave some learning for the field.

Then decide.

Go. Wait. Change. Decline.

None of those words is a measure of Christian worth.

The important question is whether the decision is faithful to the responsibility actually before you.

Preparation does not eliminate uncertainty.

It helps you become responsible inside it.

You may still feel weak when you leave.

The field will expose assumptions you did not know you held. Your language will fail. Plans will change. People will correct you. Your role may become different from the one you imagined.

None of that means preparation was wasted.

Preparation should make you capable of learning rather than pretending you have finished learning.

Christian formation remains God's work. Your effort matters, but it is not self-salvation. Your competence matters, but it does not make you sovereign over ministry fruit. Your plans matter, but they remain plans under God's providence.

After finishing this book, return to three questions:

**What responsibility is actually before me?**

**What deserves faithful attention now?**

**Who needs to help me judge the next step?**

You will never prepare yourself beyond dependence.

That was never the goal.

Christ is sufficient before you become useful. God remains sovereign when plans change. The Spirit remains at work when your competence reaches its limit. The church remains a body in which no one person carries every responsibility.

Prepare seriously because people deserve responsible service.

Hold the plan humbly because the mission does not belong to you.

Then take the next faithful step.
