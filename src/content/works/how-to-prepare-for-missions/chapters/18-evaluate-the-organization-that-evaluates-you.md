Mission organizations evaluate candidates.

Candidates and churches should evaluate organizations too.

An agency may influence role, supervision, finances, housing, care, security, team placement, family life, crisis response, and sometimes whether a worker can remain in a country.

Christian branding is not evidence that these systems are healthy.

## Trust Requires Structures

Biblical teaching on Christian leadership establishes moral boundaries. Leaders are not to dominate. Authority remains accountable. The vulnerable are not disposable.

Those texts do not directly supply a modern safeguarding policy or grievance procedure.

The procedures below are prudential and professional means of protecting responsibilities Scripture makes morally serious.

Small churches or mission networks may not possess corporate departments. That is not the standard.

The standard is whether the necessary functions exist: accountable supervision, safe reporting, competent safeguarding, fair investigation, appropriate care, and meaningful appeal.

## Doctrine Matters

Ask clearly what the organization believes, which doctrinal issues affect placement, how disagreements are handled, and what theological commitments actually govern ministry practice.

Do not assume a vague evangelical label answers everything relevant.

## Authority Must Be Visible

Who supervises you?

What authority does that person possess?

Can expectations change without agreement? What happens if you disagree? Who evaluates the supervisor? Can a worker appeal?

A healthy organization should be able to explain its authority structure without spiritualizing ordinary employment or membership decisions.

## Grievance Must Work When the Powerful Person Is the Problem

A policy matters most when the powerful person is the problem.

A worker should know where misconduct by a supervisor can be reported, whether there is a route outside the normal chain, who investigates, what protections exist against retaliation, and what happens if senior leadership is implicated.

A policy on paper is not proof of culture.

Ask how the process functions without demanding confidential case details.

## Safeguarding Is Not Optional Bureaucracy

Organizations working with children or vulnerable adults need clear safeguarding responsibilities.

This includes appropriate prevention, screening, reporting, response, investigation, external reporting where law requires it, survivor care, and recordkeeping.

Exact legal requirements differ by jurisdiction. Verify the actual requirements governing the assignment through current official or qualified professional sources.

"We handle these things biblically" is not a substitute for clear procedures.

Biblical faithfulness should produce serious protection, not procedural vagueness.

## Member Care Needs Boundaries

Pastoral care, employment supervision, clinical care, and disciplinary investigation are different functions.

A worker should understand who provides counseling, what remains confidential, what safety exceptions exist, whether counseling information can affect placement, whether an external clinician can be used, and what happens during a mental-health crisis.

Combining every function under one leader creates conflicts of interest.

## Ask About Money and Local Accountability

Understand the support model, administrative costs, emergency reserves, insurance arrangements, and what happens during support shortfalls.

There is no single biblical funding model.

Transparency matters across models.

Also ask how the organization relates to local churches, ministries, staff, professionals, and regional mission movements.

Who determines strategy? Who holds property? Who controls funds? Who evaluates expatriate workers?

Headquarters does not automatically possess better contextual judgment simply because it possesses formal authority.

## Ask About Departure

A healthy organization should be able to discuss leaving.

What happens if the role changes, the family needs to return, the worker disagrees with leadership, support collapses, health changes, the assignment ends, or misconduct occurs?

An organization that interprets every departure primarily as disloyalty should concern you.

Trust should be informed, not naive.

Selection is reciprocal because responsibility is reciprocal.

But even a healthy organization cannot erase the cost of the assignment itself.
