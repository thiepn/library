At some point the preparation process has to produce a decision.

Not perfect certainty.

A decision.

Four outcomes remain legitimate.

[[FIG07]]

## Go

The assignment is sufficiently clear. True blockers have been responsibly addressed. The church, household where relevant, organization or team, and specialists possess enough confidence to proceed.

Uncertainty remains, but it is proportionate.

"Go" can still include explicit development commitments.

## Wait

The direction may still be good, but one or more genuine blockers require attention.

Waiting should answer what you are waiting for, what evidence would change the decision, and when the decision will be reviewed.

Undefined waiting easily becomes endless delay.

## Change

The missionary aspiration may remain while the assignment changes.

Change the role, field, organization, timing, team, funding model, or other part of the plan.

This is often a sign that discernment worked.

It is not necessarily failure.

## Decline

Sometimes the correct answer is no.

Not every opportunity should become an assignment.

A Christian may decline because the role does not fit, the organization is unhealthy, household responsibilities conflict, another responsibility has priority, the context does not need the contribution, or conscience cannot support the decision.

Declining this opportunity does not mean declining Christ.

## Case A - Wait Without Abandoning the Direction

Consider a married candidate with strong church affirmation, relevant professional competence, a clearly defined organization and role, and genuine language progress.

One child, however, requires ongoing medical treatment that cannot currently be accessed safely or reliably in the proposed location.

The conclusion is not that the family lacks faith.

The health need is a genuine blocker for this assignment under current conditions.

A faithful outcome could be to wait until treatment changes, choose another location where the same professional contribution is useful, or change the role.

The missionary aspiration has not disappeared.

The assignment has been judged responsibly.

## Case B - Go While Still Incomplete

Consider a technical worker with clear professional competence, healthy church and organizational relationships, adequate household support, sufficient initial communication capacity, and protected field-language time.

The worker still lacks advanced language, deep cultural understanding, and extensive local relationships.

Those limitations matter.

They are also precisely the kinds of capacities that may require substantial field learning.

If no genuine blocker remains, the decision can be **go**, with explicit development commitments.

The worker does not need to become finished before arrival.

## Make the Decision Communally

A wise deployment decision may involve the candidate, spouse or household, church, organization, team, locally rooted Christian leaders, and professional evaluators.

Not everyone has equal authority over every question.

A physician's competence should not be decided by a pastor with no medical expertise. A professional regulator cannot decide the candidate's spiritual maturity. A mission organization should not pretend it can judge every family concern without listening to the family.

Consequential decisions deserve more than isolated intuition.

## Do Not Build a God's-Will Score

It may be tempting to create weighted categories.

Calling: 8/10. Language: 6/10. Finances: 7/10. Church approval: 9/10. Therefore: go.

Do not do this.

The appearance of mathematical objectivity hides value judgments.

One serious blocker cannot necessarily be offset by many good scores.

Christian discernment remains judgment.

## Pray Without Demanding a Sign

Ask God for wisdom. Ask for courage. Ask for correction. Ask that your desires be rightly ordered.

But do not force God to provide one unmistakable experience before you act.

Chapter 3 established that subjective guidance matters without becoming infallible.

The final decision can therefore be spiritually serious and epistemically humble.

Go, wait, change, and decline are planning categories, not divinely revealed labels. Their purpose is to ensure that discernment produces an intelligible decision rather than an endless feeling of uncertainty.

Once the decision is go, preparation changes form.

You no longer need to prove that you deserve to enter the field.

You need to enter it in the right posture.
