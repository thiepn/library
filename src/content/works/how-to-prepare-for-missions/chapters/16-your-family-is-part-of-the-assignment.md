A missionary application may have one candidate's name on it.

The consequences may be carried by several people.

A spouse may change careers. Children may change schools, languages, friendships, churches, and countries. A family may live far from grandparents. A single worker may carry responsibilities toward aging parents. A married couple may disagree about timing.

Missionary aspiration does not suspend ordinary Christian obligations to the people closest to us.

## A Spouse Is Not Support Infrastructure

One harmful missionary pattern is to treat the primary missionary as the called person while the spouse exists to enable the calling.

That may appear subtly.

The spouse's vocation is treated as optional. The spouse is expected to absorb childcare, hospitality, emotional care, home education, language learning, and team social expectations so that the "real missionary" can perform ministry.

Sometimes couples freely choose unequal vocational roles.

That can be entirely appropriate.

The problem is assuming one spouse's missionary ambition automatically determines the other's responsibility.

Missionary aspiration does not erase ordinary responsibility toward spouse, children, parents, or others genuinely entrusted to our care. Neither should one family member's hesitation be treated automatically as spiritual obstruction.

Couples from different Christian traditions will describe marital authority and decision-making differently. This book does not resolve those denominational disputes. It insists on the narrower principle necessary for missionary preparation: spiritual language must never turn a spouse or child into involuntary infrastructure for another person's ministry ambition.

## Children Carry Real Costs and Real Possibilities

Children may benefit greatly from cross-cultural life. They may develop languages, relationships, wider perspective, and adaptability.

They may also experience repeated transitions, loss, disrupted friendships, identity complexity, educational difficulty, or separation from extended family.

The research literature on third-culture and missionary children is limited and heterogeneous. A 2022 systematic review found only a small number of eligible studies, which is one reason sweeping claims should be resisted.

The ethical conclusion does not depend on proving that missionary life usually harms or usually benefits children.

Children are people entrusted to parental care.

Their needs belong inside discernment.

## Sacrifice Does Not Cancel Responsibility

Following Christ can cost families.

Scripture does not make family comfort the highest good.

But the existence of legitimate sacrifice does not allow adults to rename every preventable harm "the cost of obedience."

Parents remain parents. Spouses remain spouses. Christians retain genuine obligations toward dependent or vulnerable family members.

A family may faithfully accept major difficulty.

The decision should be truthful about who bears it.

## Household Readiness Is Specific

Consider what the assignment means for each affected person.

Does a spouse share the decision freely? What happens to each person's vocation? What education options exist? What health needs must be supported? How will household workload be divided during intensive language study? What happens during crisis? Is confidential support available to a spouse independently of the primary missionary? What happens if one member of the family needs to leave?

These questions may change the assignment.

That is legitimate.

## Singleness Has Different Questions

Single missionaries do not have fewer relational needs.

They may face isolation, assumptions about availability, expectations of endless flexibility, friendship loss, or lack of household companionship.

Organizations should not treat single workers as infinitely deployable because they have no spouse or children.

Different household states produce different responsibilities.

None makes the worker spiritually superior.

The assignment belongs inside household reality.

That brings us naturally to sustainability.
