## Group covenant

A healthy group should agree to several principles:

1. Scripture remains the primary theological authority.
2. No one is required to disclose trauma, abuse, mental health history, or private sin in order to participate.
3. Confidentiality has limits where safety, abuse, self-harm risk, or legal or safeguarding duties require action.
4. Members do not diagnose one another.
5. Disagreement should not be punished socially.
6. Leaders do not use the group to create dependence on themselves.
7. Urgent safety concerns are routed to appropriate help rather than processed only as discussion topics.

## Part I — The God We Love

- Which distorted picture of God most easily shapes Christian practice?
- Why is accurate doctrine necessary but insufficient for love?
- How do the two greatest commands resist private spirituality?
- What changes when grace is placed before response?

**Practice:** Choose one biblical truth about God and one concrete act of neighbor-love that follows from it.

## Part II — Grace Creates a New Godward Life

- Why is the problem deeper than weak habits?
- What is gained by describing new life as a cluster rather than a rigid ladder?
- How do justification and adoption differ?
- Which of the four assurance lenses do you neglect or misuse?

**Safeguard:** Do not turn the discussion into public judgments about who is or is not converted.

## Part III — The Shape and Growth of Love

- How can obedience be necessary without becoming merit?
- Why is emotional intensity an unreliable standalone measure?
- Which practices help faith without becoming mechanisms?
- Where do legalism and emotionalism mirror each other?

**Practice:** Make one practice smaller, clearer, and connected to an actual purpose.

## Part IV — Love Nourished in the Church

- How is a body different from an audience?
- What modes of Scripture engagement besides private reading are biblical?
- What does lament add to prayer?
- What common ground can Protestants affirm about baptism and the Supper despite disagreement?

**Safeguard:** If discussion reveals coercive leadership or serious harm, do not pressure the person to reconcile in the group. Use appropriate safety routes.

## Part V — Love Under Pressure

- Why is dryness a description rather than a diagnosis?
- Which false explanations for suffering have you heard?
- How can rival loves be diagnosed without calling every desire idolatry?
- What does Scripture actually name as the ordinary weapons of spiritual warfare?

**Practice:** Identify one pressure and choose a proportionate response rather than a total life overhaul.

## Part VI — Love Moves Toward Others

- How do universal love and differentiated responsibility fit together?
- Why must forgiveness, reconciliation, trust, and access be distinguished?
- What makes ordinary work an arena of neighbor-love?
- What limits Christian authority?
- Why do deeds and gospel speech need distinction without divorce?
- How can money distort cross-cultural partnership?

**Safeguard:** Do not use hypothetical discussion to tell someone in an abuse situation what they “must” do. Encourage competent local help.

## Part VII — The Endurance and Fulfillment of Love

- What is the difference between perseverance and maintaining a spiritual mood?
- How should Protestant disagreement about final apostasy be handled pastorally?
- What does bodily resurrection correct about popular ideas of heaven?
- How does new creation reshape present obedience?

**Practice:** Name one form of hidden faithfulness that matters in your current season.
