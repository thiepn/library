Christian hope culminates in resurrection, judgment, new creation, God's dwelling with his people, the removal of death and curse, and life in the presence of God. Its destination is richer than the escape of a soul from the material world.

This is the destination toward which every earlier chapter has been moving. Love is commanded now, received by grace, practiced imperfectly, tested under pressure, and finally made complete by God.

## New creation is the final owner

Revelation 21–22 provides the primary final horizon. The biblical story ends not with creation discarded but with heaven and earth renewed, the holy city, God's dwelling with humanity, the defeat of death and mourning, the removal of curse, and the servants of God seeing his face and serving him.

These images are apocalyptic and must be read according to their genre. They should not be flattened into a literal architectural diagram of eternity. Yet the theological claims are substantial: embodied life matters, creation matters, community matters, holiness matters, God's presence matters, and evil does not remain forever.

Christian hope is embodied, communal, holy life in the presence of God, far richer than either disembodied escape or the endless extension of present religious activity.

## Resurrection, not absorption

The New Testament's resurrection hope preserves creaturely identity. Christ is bodily raised, and believers await resurrection as redeemed creatures who remain distinct from the God they worship.

Language of union, participation, or even *theosis* in some Christian traditions belongs within the enduring Creator-creature distinction. Protestants differ on how freely they use such terms; the promise is communion with God, not becoming God by nature.

Perfected communion means unbroken fellowship with God as redeemed creatures: knowing, worshiping, serving, receiving, and loving without sin.

## Knowing God without becoming omniscient

First Corinthians 13 contrasts present partial knowledge with future completion and uses the language of knowing in relation to being known. The passage promises profound future completeness while leaving redeemed creatures finite rather than omniscient.

Glory does not erase creaturely finitude. Its goodness does not depend on imagining either static boredom or endless information acquisition. The biblical promise is sufficient: we will be with the Lord, raised incorruptible, free from sin and death, and fully fitted for communion with God.

Our present questions about exactly how learning, discovery, memory, culture, and creaturely development function in the new creation should be held with humility.

## Seeing Christ and becoming like him

First John 3 connects the future appearing of Christ, seeing him, and being like him. The text gives a real promise of future likeness without erasing the distinction between Savior and redeemed people.

This hope also has present moral force: those who hope in Christ purify themselves. Eschatology is not an escape from holiness. The future shape of love gives direction to present life.

What grace begins, God will bring to completion. The Christian will not forever be divided between desire and obedience, love and fear, trust and doubt. Sin will no longer distort the heart.

## Every nation before God

Revelation's multinational scenes show peoples and languages gathered in worship. The global horizon is not decorative diversity added to an otherwise individual salvation story.

The new creation judges every sinful distortion of culture while refusing the idea that one present culture becomes the eternal human norm. The nations bring their glory into the holy city under God's reign. Whatever those images imply in detail, they resist both nationalism and cultural flattening.

The church's present cross-cultural life should anticipate that future with humility. No region owns Christianity. No ethnicity is nearer to God by nature. The Lamb gathers one people from many peoples.

## Love without sin

What would it mean to love God without sin?

It would mean no rival throne in the heart, no desire to use God as a means, no resentment of holiness, no self-righteous bargaining, no contempt for neighbor, no envy, no hidden exploitation, no fear of condemnation, no spiritual boredom produced by sin, no divided allegiance.

It would also mean love for other redeemed people without rivalry, manipulation, racism, abuse, or fear. Justice and mercy would no longer seem competitors. Worship and daily life would no longer be separated by sin.

Here *perfected communion* names that reality. The phrase is a theological synthesis, not a technical expression taken from one verse. It gathers the biblical destination: God with his people in resurrection life, seeing and serving him, holy and whole.

## Hope for present love

The future keeps present practice in perspective. God brings his people into the new creation; they do not perfect themselves into it. Every chapter on practice, repentance, growth, mission, and perseverance remains penultimate.

That does not make obedience small. It makes it hopeful. We practice now because grace has claimed us and the future belongs to God. We repent because sin will not define the end. We love neighbors because the coming kingdom is not a private spiritual escape. We endure because resurrection means loss is not final. We engage mission because Christ is gathering his people. We worship because God is worthy now and will be enjoyed without sin then.

The final answer to *How do I love God?* is therefore not a technique. Know him as he has revealed himself. Receive his prior grace in Christ. Walk by the Spirit. Trust, desire, revere, obey, and cling to him. Love the people he gives you. Remain faithful in the church without surrendering conscience to human domination. Lament when life hurts. Resist rivals and spiritual opposition. Speak the gospel. Serve humbly. Persevere.

And remember that the deepest completion of this love will be God's work.

### Response

Set your hope on resurrection and new creation. Let the promise of God's presence loosen the grip of rival goods and make present obedience meaningful. You will remain a redeemed creature, raised and made holy, sharing perfected communion with God and his people rather than becoming omniscient, divine by nature, or absorbed into God. Bring today's unfinished love to Christ. Worship now, serve now, repent now, love now—and let the future promise free you from trying to manufacture heaven inside your present emotional state.
