This guide is descriptive and non-diagnostic. “God feels distant” can describe many states. Use it to choose proportionate care without claiming certainty you do not possess.

## 1. Name the experience

Ask:

- What exactly feels different: prayer, pleasure, attention, hope, motivation, trust, church participation, or everything?
- How long has it been present?
- Is the experience limited to explicitly spiritual activities or broad across life?
- Is daily functioning significantly impaired?
- Is there known grief, conflict, illness, sleep loss, trauma, medication change, or major stress?
- Is there clear, known sin that requires repentance?

Do not treat these questions as a diagnostic test.

## 2. Check safety and function first

If there is immediate danger, self-harm risk, abuse, inability to meet basic needs, severe functional decline, psychosis-like symptoms, or another urgent crisis, seek appropriate emergency, medical, mental health, safeguarding, or civil help. A spiritual interpretation should not delay urgent protection.

Specific crisis and safeguarding services vary by jurisdiction. Use competent local resources.

## 3. Consider possible domains

**Known sin or deliberate neglect** — Repent where conduct is actually clear.

**Grief and suffering** — Lament; reduce pressure to perform; receive community support.

**Exhaustion or bodily illness** — Rest and seek medical care as appropriate.

**Depression or broad loss of interest or pleasure** — Consider professional assessment, especially when persistent or impairing. Do not diagnose yourself from this appendix.

**Scrupulosity or intrusive thoughts** — Do not equate unwanted thoughts with chosen allegiance. Reduce repetitive reassurance-seeking and seek appropriate specialized help if the pattern is consuming.

**Doubt** — Specify the question. Intellectual doubt may need study and conversation; emotional distress may need different care.

**Church injury or coercion** — Safety and trustworthy community may need attention before increased church exposure.

**Mixed or unknown causes** — Mixed causes are common. “I do not know” is an acceptable answer.

### Discernment matrix

Several domains may coexist, and the cause may remain partly unknown. This table is descriptive, not diagnostic.

| Possible domain | Possible indicators | Proportionate response | Do **not** infer automatically |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Known sin / deliberate neglect** | Conduct is concrete and morally clear | Repent, confess appropriately, repair where needed | That every low feeling proves hidden sin |
| **Grief / loss** | Bereavement, disappointment, communal or personal loss | Lament, receive support, reduce pressure to perform | That grief equals unbelief |
| **Body / fatigue / illness** | Sleep loss, exhaustion, illness, depleted concentration | Rest, attend to bodily needs, seek medical care where appropriate | That bodily weakness is spiritual failure |
| **Depression / anhedonia** | Broad, persistent loss of interest or pleasure; impaired functioning | Seek qualified assessment when persistent or impairing; keep spiritual practices simple | That low spiritual pleasure proves lack of faith |
| **Scrupulosity / anxiety** | Intrusive doubts, repeated checking, reassurance-seeking | Reduce compulsive checking; seek appropriate specialized care if consuming | That unwanted thoughts reveal chosen allegiance |
| **Doubt** | Specific intellectual, theological, or existential questions | Name the question; study and seek wise conversation | That every question is rebellion |
| **Church injury / coercion** | Fear, manipulation, abuse, retaliation, unsafe leadership | Seek safety, trustworthy support, safeguarding or civil help where appropriate | That greater exposure to the harmful setting is always the faithful response |
| **Mixed / unknown** | Multiple factors or no clear explanation | Use proportionate care, lament, wait patiently, remain connected | That uncertainty must be filled with a confident spiritual diagnosis |

## 4. Choose proportionate responses

Possible responses include:

- repent where sin is known;
- keep prayer and Scripture simple;
- remain connected to trustworthy people;
- rest;
- seek medical or mental health assessment;
- address practical stressors;
- seek safety from harmful relationships or leadership;
- lament;
- reduce compulsive checking;
- wait patiently without inventing a cause.

No sequence guarantees restored feeling.

## 5. What not to conclude automatically

Do not conclude automatically that:

- God has abandoned you;
- you are unsaved because affect is low;
- a hidden sin must explain the experience;
- depression proves weak faith;
- intrusive thoughts reveal your true allegiance;
- more devotional quantity will necessarily fix the problem;
- another person's confident spiritual explanation is correct;
- medication or therapy should be stopped in order to demonstrate faith.
