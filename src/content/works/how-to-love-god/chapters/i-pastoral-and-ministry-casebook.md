All cases below are fictional composites created for teaching. They do not diagnose real people and do not prescribe one universal response.

## Case 1 — Dryness and depression

Mara has been a Christian for years. Over two months she loses interest not only in prayer but also food, friends, music, and work. She sleeps poorly, struggles to concentrate, and begins missing responsibilities. A well-meaning friend tells her she has “left her first love” and needs a stricter quiet time.

**Discernment:** The broad loss of interest and functional change should not be reduced to spiritual dryness. Known sin can still be addressed if present, but competent clinical assessment is appropriate. Prayer, simple Scripture, and church support can continue without becoming the sole treatment.

## Case 2 — Scrupulosity and intrusive thoughts

Daniel experiences unwanted blasphemous thoughts during prayer. He repeatedly confesses them, asks three pastors whether he is saved, and searches the same theological question online late into the night. Reassurance helps for minutes, then doubt returns.

**Discernment:** Unwanted thoughts are not identical to chosen allegiance. Repeated reassurance may be participating in a compulsive cycle. Pastoral care should point to Christ without endlessly answering the same test and should consider referral to a clinician experienced with OCD, including scrupulosity.

## Case 3 — Grief and unanswered prayer

A family prays for a child's recovery from severe illness. The child dies. Members of the church offer explanations: not enough faith, a hidden purpose that will soon become obvious, demonic attack, or special selection for a spiritual lesson.

**Discernment:** The family does not need speculative certainty. Lament, presence, practical care, and resurrection hope are appropriate. Specific causal claims require warrant that the situation does not provide.

## Case 4 — Forgiveness without restored trust

A business partner steals money, lies repeatedly, then apologizes after evidence becomes undeniable. He asks his Christian partner to prove forgiveness by restoring account access immediately.

**Discernment:** Forgiveness does not require immediate trust or access. Repentance should include truthful accounting, restitution, and acceptance of consequences. Financial controls can remain while the offended Christian rejects personal vengeance.

## Case 5 — Church harm and safeguarding

A youth volunteer reports that a senior leader has been sending sexual messages to a teenager. Another elder insists the family must first meet the leader privately under Matthew 18 so the church's reputation is not damaged.

**Discernment:** This is not an ordinary conflict process. Safeguarding and applicable civil reporting requirements take priority over a private confrontation rule. The accused leader should not control the investigation. The teenager should not be pressured to meet the leader or forgive publicly.

## Case 6 — Mission power and cross-cultural misunderstanding

A foreign-funded ministry employs local pastors. The donor organization invites “honest feedback,” but local leaders know that contracts are renewed annually and that previous critics lost opportunities. Foreign staff conclude that unanimous agreement proves the strategy is locally owned.

**Discernment:** Formal consultation does not guarantee free consent when money and status create dependency. The organization should create safer channels for dissent, clarify decision rights, reduce retaliation risk, and treat local leaders as theological and strategic partners rather than implementers.

## Case 7 — Spiritual-warfare fear

A family experiences repeated illness and financial trouble. A church member identifies an elderly relative as the likely source of a curse and recommends an aggressive deliverance meeting in which the relative will be confronted.

**Discernment:** Scripture does not authorize speculative identification of a supposed witch from misfortune. Medical, financial, relational, and spiritual care can proceed without accusation. Coercive deliverance practices are unacceptable. Protect the vulnerable person and address concrete problems with appropriate evidence.
