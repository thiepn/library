This guide is not a salvation test. It is designed to help ordinary self-examination remain bounded.

## Step 1 — Begin outside yourself

State the gospel in simple terms: your hope rests on Christ, not on your ability to inspect your heart perfectly. Read a substantial gospel passage rather than searching for a phrase that produces reassurance.

## Step 2 — Remember adoption and the Spirit

Christian assurance includes filial relation. Pray as a child coming through Christ. Do not require a particular emotional sensation as proof of the Spirit.

## Step 3 — Use external gospel reminders

Receive Scripture, preaching, Christian fellowship, baptism, and the Lord's Supper according to the faithful practice of your church. These direct attention beyond private introspection.

## Step 4 — Examine fruit at pattern level

Ask a small number of questions:

- Do I continue returning to Christ?
- Is there real repentance when sin becomes clear?
- Is obedience present in pattern, though imperfect?
- Is love for other people becoming more concrete?
- Do I take biblical warnings seriously rather than dismissing them?

Do not assign numerical scores.

## Step 5 — Stop

If the same question immediately demands another round of checking, do not assume more analysis will produce spiritual certainty. Repeated motive inspection and reassurance-seeking can become compulsive.

If religious doubt, intrusive thoughts, confession, or checking is consuming substantial time or impairing daily life, consider specialized mental health support alongside pastoral care.

## Crisis note

If self-examination is accompanied by self-harm thoughts, inability to function safely, abuse, or another urgent crisis, seek appropriate immediate local help. Do not use this appendix as the primary response.
