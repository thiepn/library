This appendix groups passages by the role they play in the book. It is not a list of isolated proof texts. Read each passage in its literary context and, where possible, within the argument of the biblical book.

## God, revelation, and wholehearted love

**Deuteronomy 6:4–9** — The Shema joins confession of the one Lord with wholehearted love and embeds that devotion in household, memory, teaching, and ordinary life.

**Exodus 34:5–8** — A foundational Old Testament disclosure of God's character, holding mercy, covenant faithfulness, forgiveness, and justice together.

**Psalm 73:23–28** — God as portion and good even amid bodily weakness; supports supreme value without contempt for created goods.

**Matthew 22:34–40; Mark 12:28–34** — Jesus identifies love for God as first and love for neighbor as second and corresponding; the commands are distinct yet coordinated.

**John 1:1–18; Colossians 1:15–20** — Christ's centrality in revealing the invisible God and the Christian confession of his cosmic and saving significance.

## Heart renewal, grace, and new life

**Deuteronomy 30:1–10** — Divine heart-transformation oriented toward love for God. Do not make the text a complete New Testament ordo of regeneration.

**Jeremiah 31:31–34** — New covenant, internalized instruction, covenant knowledge, belonging, and forgiveness. The passage contributes its own terms rather than serving as a generic “new birth” text.

**Ezekiel 36:22–28** — Cleansing, new heart, new spirit, God's Spirit, and resulting obedience.

**John 3:1–8** — New birth and the Spirit; divine initiative and the necessity of life from above.

**Romans 5:1–11** — Justification, peace with God, hope, suffering, the Spirit, and God's love demonstrated in Christ. Romans 5:5 is best used primarily for God's love for us made known through the Spirit.

**Romans 8:1–39** — No condemnation, Spirit, mortification, adoption, suffering, prayer, divine purpose, and security in God's love.

**Ephesians 2:1–10** — Grace, faith, salvation apart from works as ground, and good works as the life for which believers are created.

## Assurance and communion

**John 14:15–24** — Love for Christ, obedience, the Spirit, and relational communion. Do not turn obedience into the purchase price of saving love.

**John 15:1–11** — Abiding, dependence, fruit, obedience, love, and joy.

**1 John 1:1–2:2** — Fellowship, walking in light, confession, forgiveness, and Christ's advocacy.

**1 John 3:14–24; 4:7–21** — Love of brothers and sisters, assurance, God's prior love, and the relation between claimed God-love and visible neighbor-love.

## Obedience, affection, and growth

**Psalm 63** — Desire and longing for God.

**Romans 12** — Mercies of God leading to embodied worship, renewed thinking, gifts, love, doing good to enemies, and whole-life obedience.

**Galatians 5:13–26** — Spirit, flesh, freedom, love, fruit, and real moral conflict.

**Philippians 2:12–13** — Human obedience and God's work held together; do not turn later slogans about grace and effort into Paul's wording.

**Colossians 3:1–17** — Attention to Christ, putting sin to death, new communal virtues, forgiveness, Scripture, song, and gratitude.

## Church, Word, prayer, baptism, and Supper

**Acts 2:37–47** — Baptism, teaching, fellowship, prayer, table, generosity, and shared life.

**1 Corinthians 10:16–17** — The Lord's Supper as participation or fellowship connected with the unity of the one body.

**1 Corinthians 11:17–34** — Received tradition, remembrance, proclamation, gathered accountability, self-examination, and discernment in the Supper.

**1 Corinthians 12:12–27** — The church as one body with differentiated members.

**Colossians 3:16** — The Word dwelling richly through teaching, admonition, and song.

**Hebrews 10:19–25** — Access through Christ, holding hope, gathering, and mutual encouragement toward love and good works.

**Matthew 6:9–13** — Jesus' model prayer: God's name and kingdom, provision, forgiveness, and protection.

## Dryness, suffering, and lament

**Psalm 42–43** — Spiritual thirst, memory, grief, self-address, and hope without a simple diagnostic claim.

**Psalm 88** — Lament that does not resolve into felt consolation before the poem ends.

**Job 1–2; 38–42** — Suffering that exposes the inadequacy of simple retribution and creaturely claims to exhaustive explanation.

**John 9:1–5** — Jesus rejects the proposed direct sin-to-suffering explanation in the case presented.

**2 Corinthians 12:7–10** — Repeated unanswered petition met with sustaining grace; a strong ordinary Christian example.

**Hebrews 12:5–11** — Fatherly discipline and formation; not authorization for hidden-sin diagnosis from every hardship.

**1 Peter 1:3–9** — Grief in trials, hope, faith, and love for Christ together.

## Neighbor, forgiveness, authority, and witness

**Luke 10:25–37** — Neighbor-love as costly enacted mercy across a social boundary.

**Matthew 5:43–48; Romans 12:14–21** — Enemy-love, prayer, non-retaliation, and overcoming evil with good.

**Matthew 18:15–20** — Church reconciliation and discipline; not a secrecy rule requiring private confrontation before safeguarding or reporting possible abuse or crime.

**1 Corinthians 5** — Serious church discipline. Do not assume without argument that the offender is identical to the person discussed in 2 Corinthians 2.

**Mark 10:42–45; 1 Peter 5:1–4** — Leadership contrasted with domination; shepherding under Christ.

**Matthew 28:18–20** — Commission to the eleven with a continuing ecclesial disciple-making dimension; not proof that every individual has an identical missionary vocation.

**1 Peter 3:15–16; Colossians 4:2–6** — Ordinary Christian readiness and wise speech toward outsiders.

## Perseverance and new creation

**Philippians 1:3–11** — Confidence in God's completing work; important but not a standalone settlement of the perseverance dispute.

**Jude 20–25** — Human continuing and divine keeping held together; interpretive nuance remains around the phrase concerning God's love.

**Revelation 2:1–7** — Real decline and call to return; the precise referent of “first love” is debated.

**1 Corinthians 15** — Bodily resurrection and victory over death.

**1 John 3:1–3** — Present identity as God's children, future likeness to Christ, seeing him, and present purification.

**Revelation 21–22** — Primary final horizon: new creation, God's dwelling, death and curse removed, service, face-to-face communion, and hope.
