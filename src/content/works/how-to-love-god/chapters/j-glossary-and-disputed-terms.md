**Adoption** — God's receiving of believers as his children, bringing filial belonging, access, inheritance, and the Spirit's ministry. Related to but distinct from justification.

**Assurance** — Christian confidence concerning salvation. The discussion uses four lenses: Christ and God's promise as controlling ground; the Spirit and adoption; external gospel means in the church; and non-meritorious fruit at pattern level.

**Baptism** — Christ-instituted, triune, initiatory, ecclesial practice connected biblically with discipleship and new-life identity. Protestant traditions disagree on subjects, mode, and efficacy.

**Boundary** — A modern prudential term for relational limits on access, contact, responsibility, or behavior. Not a biblical technical word; boundaries must be evaluated morally rather than assumed good by definition.

**Communion with God** — Lived fellowship with the triune God: access to the Father through the Son in the Spirit, including prayer, abiding, worship, obedience, and filial relation. Felt communion is not the ground of justification.

**Contextualization** — Communicating and embodying Christian truth intelligibly within a culture while Scripture judges both local and missionary cultural assumptions.

**Evangelism** — Intelligible communication of the gospel with the aim of calling people to repentance and faith in Christ. Related to but not identical with mercy or social action.

**Filial fear** — A traditional term used to distinguish reverent childlike fear of God from servile fear or terror of condemnation. The distinction is theological shorthand rather than one biblical technical phrase.

**Forgiveness** — The Christian response that rejects vengeance and extends mercy regarding wrongdoing. Christians disagree about the precise relation of completed forgiveness to an offender's repentance. Forgiveness is not identical to reconciliation, trust, access, or removal of consequences.

**Justification** — God's gracious declaration of the believer's righteous standing because of Christ and through faith, not because of the merit of love or obedience. Protestant traditions differ on some detailed formulations while sharing a strong grace-and-faith center.

**Means of grace** — A tradition-sensitive phrase for God-given means through which believers receive and are nourished by grace. Some traditions use it narrowly for Word and sacraments; others more broadly. The book avoids using the phrase as a claim that practices mechanically produce affection.

**Mission** — The church's participation in God's sending purpose, including witness and disciple-making and, in specialized forms, cross-cultural sending. The term is broader than one modern agency structure.

**Missionary** — A useful vocational term for people specially sent for mission work, often across significant cultural or linguistic boundaries. Every Christian participates in witness; not every Christian needs the same vocational label.

**Ordered and disordered love** — A historic theological framework especially associated with Augustinian reflection: created goods are loved rightly under God and wrongly when given ultimate place. Useful synthesis, not direct biblical wording.

**Perfect love and Christian perfection** — In Wesleyan-Holiness traditions, language for a present work or state of mature, wholehearted love understood in ways that usually distinguish it from absolute sinlessness or omniscience. Other Protestants disagree. The present-life dispute is distinguished here from eschatological perfected love in resurrection.

**Reconciliation** — Restored relationship after alienation. Unlike unilateral refusal of vengeance, reconciliation normally requires sufficient truth, safety, and mutuality.

**Sanctification** — God's gracious work by which believers, actively walking by the Spirit, grow in holiness. It is distinct from justification and does not become meritorious self-salvation.

**Spiritual abuse** — A contemporary pastoral and interdisciplinary term for coercive misuse of spiritual authority, divine claims, Scripture, community pressure, or religious belonging to dominate others. It should not be used as a synonym for every painful church disagreement.

**Spiritual dryness** — A descriptive term for low felt desire, consolation, or nearness to God. It does not diagnose its own cause.

**Theosis and participation** — Terms used in several Christian traditions for sharing in divine life by grace. Orthodox Christian use preserves the Creator-creature distinction; redeemed humans do not become God by nature.

**Trust** — Confidence in another person's reliability or safety. Trust can be damaged and may need evidence and time to rebuild; forgiveness does not automatically restore it.

**Unreached people group** — A modern strategic category used in mission research, defined differently by organizations. It can help identify access barriers but is not an inspired biblical taxonomy of the nations.
