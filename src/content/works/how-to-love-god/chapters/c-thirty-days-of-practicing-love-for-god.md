This thirty-day guide is an organizational tool, not a promise of transformation within thirty days. Completion does not prove maturity. Missed days are not failure. Adapt the sequence to your schedule, health, disability, family responsibilities, work, safety, and church access.

Each day has four simple movements: **Receive** a biblical truth; **Respond** in prayer; **Practice** one concrete act; **Release** the outcome to God.

## Days 1–7 — Know and receive

**Day 1 — The God who reveals himself.** Read Exodus 34:5–8. Identify what the passage actually says about God's character. Pray one truth back to God.

**Day 2 — Christ makes the Father known.** Read John 1:1–18. Attend to what the passage says about the Son and revelation. Thank God for making himself known.

**Day 3 — Whole-person love.** Read Deuteronomy 6:4–9. Choose one ordinary part of life—speech, home, work, money, body—and place it consciously under God's lordship.

**Day 4 — God and neighbor.** Read Matthew 22:34–40. Do one concrete good for a person you would normally overlook.

**Day 5 — Grace comes first.** Read Romans 5:6–11. Name one way you try to prove yourself to God. Renounce the bargain.

**Day 6 — New life.** Read John 3:1–8 and Romans 8:1–17. Ask the Spirit to form a real Godward life rather than a religious image.

**Day 7 — Accepted in Christ.** Read Ephesians 2:1–10. Restate the difference between grace as ground and obedience as fruit.

## Days 8–14 — Obey and desire

**Day 8 — One clear command.** Choose one command of Christ you know you are avoiding. Obey it without using obedience as payment.

**Day 9 — Hate evil, love people.** Read Romans 12:9–21. Identify where moral conviction is becoming contempt or where niceness is avoiding truth.

**Day 10 — Desire without pretending.** Read Psalm 63. Tell God honestly what you desire and ask for deeper desire for him.

**Day 11 — Low affect.** If you feel flat, do not manufacture emotion. Practice one small act of truth, prayer, or obedience.

**Day 12 — Grace-enabled effort.** Read Philippians 2:12–13. Choose a small practice that supports faith for the next week.

**Day 13 — Mortification.** Identify one sinful pattern and remove one concrete support it relies on.

**Day 14 — Repentance.** Confess one known sin specifically. Make repair where appropriate, then stop trying to pay for forgiveness.

## Days 15–21 — Word, prayer, and church

**Day 15 — Hear Scripture.** Listen to a substantial biblical passage rather than reading silently.

**Day 16 — Read in context.** Read one whole chapter of Scripture and summarize its argument before applying a verse.

**Day 17 — Pray the Lord's Prayer slowly.** Let each movement widen your concerns.

**Day 18 — Lament.** Read Psalm 13 or Psalm 88. Bring one unresolved pain to God without forcing resolution.

**Day 19 — Encourage.** Contact one Christian and give specific, truthful encouragement.

**Day 20 — Gather.** Participate in your church's worship if you are able and safe to do so. Focus on God and the body rather than evaluating the experience.

**Day 21 — Remember the gospel together.** Reflect on baptism or the Lord's Supper according to your church's tradition and how these practices direct believers toward Christ and the church.

## Days 22–27 — Pressure and rival loves

**Day 22 — Name dryness without diagnosing it.** If God feels distant, list what is known and unknown. Do not fill the unknowns with accusation.

**Day 23 — Care for the body.** Ask whether sleep, food, illness, movement, medication questions, or stress need appropriate attention. Seek professional help where warranted rather than self-prescribing.

**Day 24 — A rival promise.** Name one created good that promises too much. What is it promising? What obedience would place it under God?

**Day 25 — Digital wisdom.** If relevant, change one digital condition that repeatedly undermines attention or holiness. If digital life is not your major pressure, choose another environment instead.

**Day 26 — Spiritual warfare.** Read Ephesians 6:10–20. Practice the ordinary means named there. Refuse speculative causal claims.

**Day 27 — Stand with others.** Ask one trusted Christian to pray with you about a real area of pressure.

## Days 28–30 — Neighbor, witness, hope

**Day 28 — Seek a neighbor's true good.** Offer practical help, truth, protection, generosity, or presence according to a real need.

**Day 29 — Speak of Christ.** In an appropriate relationship, say something intelligible about why your hope is in Christ. Do not force the conversation.

**Day 30 — New creation.** Read Revelation 21–22. Write one sentence describing how resurrection hope changes one present fear, desire, or responsibility.

At the end, do not score yourself. Ask instead: What helped me receive truth? What became mechanical? Where did love become more concrete? What needs repentance or support? What simple practice should continue, and what should be released?
